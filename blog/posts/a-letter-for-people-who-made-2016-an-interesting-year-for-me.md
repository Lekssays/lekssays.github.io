At this particular moment in my life, I feel I have to write these lines: to evaluate my performance in all its aspects, and to thank the people who made this year interesting for me. The bad ones are not worth the time or energy it would take to thank them.

## Evaluating the Year

Before thanking the people who made my year interesting, I would like to evaluate my performance across every area.

- **Academically**, it was a good year, though a challenging one. I had to deal with many things in parallel, which was very difficult for me. In the end, however, I managed it, and my overall performance here was good. I met my own standard of satisfaction, which is the most important thing for me right now.

- **Educationally**, I had a good experience teaching some high school students the basics of web development. It was rewarding because it sat at the intersection of two fields I love. I met new people with great ideas, and I feel happy whenever one of them tells me they benefited from what I shared. That said, I would like to offer my deep apology to any of them if I overreacted at any point, either during or after the events.

  I also wrote a paper on iOS security architecture, and it was accepted for OWASP AppSec Africa 2017 in Casablanca, Morocco. It is a great honor for me to speak at this international conference, hosted by a leading organization in cyber security.

  In competitive programming, my level improved from last year to 2016. It was not quite what I had hoped for, but I believe it is acceptable in the end.

- **Socially**, it was a bad year, because I overreacted at scattered moments throughout it. I overreacted in situations that did not deserve a second thought. Even so, I tried to be more disciplined in different situations, and that was key to saving my academic semester in the end. "Life is binary: you either get 0 or 1" is the quote that explains my behavior.

- **Emotionally**, this took a toll and caused me some emotional problems during the year, so it was a bad year in this respect. Still, I learned a great deal from the experience, which is a good thing in the end.

I learned things from you that go beyond computer science. I learned lessons about life that, in some way, shaped my behavior.

I wish you all a happy new year! And keep coding, for those of you who love it!
