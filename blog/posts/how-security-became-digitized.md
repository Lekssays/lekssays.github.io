## Your Digital Life Is on Display

Nowadays, cyber security has become the equivalent of physical security. If a cyber criminal steals your passwords or your credit cards, it is as if he stole your keys or your wallet in the street. The problem with the internet is that the “wallet” is on display to everyone. It can be seen by all users, and if an attacker gains access to your online accounts, your digital life ends up posted on websites that build their business on stories like yours. Paying with credit cards online is like walking down the street and announcing to thieves: “Hi, I am here!”. The parts of your digital life that tend to interest attackers include your social network messages, your emails, your pictures, your location, and your personal information (Dejoie, Fawler, & Paradice, 1991).

## The Subfields of Cyber Security

We can see clearly, then, that cyber security is a large field within computer science, one that encompasses several subfields. For instance:

- **Cryptography** encrypts your passwords and your connections to external servers.
- **Malware analysis** stops the spread of malware online by analyzing how it behaves under certain conditions.
- **Application security** tackles potential security issues in web, mobile, and network applications.

Together, these subfields aim to secure the different aspects of your digital life and protect your privacy from attackers.

## “Attacker” Versus “Hacker”

I have used the word “attacker” since the beginning of this article because it is technically more precise than “hacker”. Hackers are not all the same. Society tends to prejudge them as unsocial (Graham, 2011), portraying them as creatures who believe in no mercy. The notion that they enjoy destroying things is an illusion that resides in filmmakers’ minds; they promote it in their movies to make them more entertaining to watch. Hackers are always cast as magicians, forever sitting in front of black screens that scroll green text. As you read these lines, you can probably recall a movie that describes a hacker in exactly this way. Surprisingly, hackers are ordinary people who live ordinary lives, just like yours and mine. They are simply more curious and more daring. They also dedicate more time to looking for new bugs and reading about new technologies, since this is a field that evolves quickly (Graham, 2011).

## Three Types of Hackers

Technically, hackers fall into three main categories depending on the primary purpose of their cyber activities: white, grey, and black hats. The colors are meaningful:

1. **White hats** report bugs and secure applications for the sake of protecting humanity.
2. **Grey hats** weigh what they should include in a report when a security issue is found. They tend to withhold some information for illegal and unethical use.
3. **Black hats** enjoy defacing websites, selling credit cards, and getting paid to hack users online (Thomas, 2001).

So the concept of ethics is fundamental in cyber security. An ethical hacker is always a white hat, one who does not wait for awards or recognition before protecting users online. Ethics are the lines that separate the different types of hackers. In other words, the color of the hat depends on the importance of ethics in the hacker’s behavior.
