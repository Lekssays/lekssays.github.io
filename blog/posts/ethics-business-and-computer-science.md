I am writing this post after a long discussion in class with a computer science professor here at Al Akhawayn University. I decided to write it to share with the world how I see ethics and business in the context of computer science.

## Computer Science Is First About Ethics

Computer science is, first and foremost, about ethics. It is about sharing knowledge and code with the world without expecting anything in return. Computer science should not be interpreted as a market, nor seen as business and money. It is more than that. It is what gathers open source programmers from all around the globe to build something exceptional that improves technology and makes people's lives easier. I am talking here about Linux, an extraordinary project that changed computing history.

## Open Source Is Not a Business

We discussed the idea of using these open source projects commercially, which was the argument for considering computer science a business. I did not agree, because the people who developed these things are fully aware of how they will be used: their projects are licensed under one of the public licenses commonly used in the field. The most famous is the GNU Public License, which, across all its versions, establishes an ethical contract between the programmer and the user. I did not use the term "client" here, because it is not defined in the open source software dictionary.

## Linux Is Widely Used

They argued about the use of Linux today, claiming that Windows is more widely used than Linux-based operating systems. However, they were not aware that Android is also a Linux-based operating system. In addition, there are now more smartphones than computers. Hence, we can conclude that Linux-based operating systems are the most popular and widely used.

## Treating Computer Science as a Business Lowers Quality

An important point to mention is that thinking of computer science as a business lowers the quality of projects. I saw a hackathon run by a Moroccan company here at Al Akhawayn University. It illustrates how people in this happy land think about money more than quality and ethics. They are selling an illusion to people in the framing of hackathons, under the umbrella of "Entrepreneurs." They were using these events to test their platform for free, even setting aside the quality of the platform itself. The strange thing is that no one asked them about their background or their knowledge in the field. Logically, someone who is going to judge your application should be more knowledgeable than you, at least in that specific field. These people are a clear example of applying business to computer science, which is bad to some extent, especially when it comes to incompetent people. Linus Torvalds's famous quote applies strongly in this context: "Talking is cheap. Show me the code."

## We Do It for Love

So, if Linus Torvalds had wanted to sell Linux, we would not have achieved what we have been achieving. Open source projects taught me to be generous, to love the community, and to help people I do not know simply because they are human. The important thing is that they are trying to improve people's lives.

You should just get the point: we are not doing this for fame or money. We are doing it for love.
