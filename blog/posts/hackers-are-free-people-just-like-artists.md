## Hackers in the Developing World

In third-world countries, hackers are treated as criminals regardless of how ethical they are. They are not legally protected, and they are seen as unwanted creatures working against the country. In Morocco, however, hackers have built a strong reputation around the globe as intelligent people who discover bugs in famous platforms and who attack individuals or groups working against the country. They form an unofficial cyber army for Morocco, defending their nation's image and rights against its enemies.

This raises the question of ethics: how ethical are they if they attack their enemies' websites? In the end, they act out of love for their country (Dejoie, Fawler, & Paradice, 1991). Yet they are still treated as enemies and criminals, because of a lack of awareness about digital threats and the absence of a forward-looking digital strategy on the part of the government.

## Hackers in Developed Countries

In developed countries, hackers are important elements in the digital strategies of companies and governments. For instance, Facebook, Twitter, Google, and other companies and universities pay white hats who find a security issue in one of their applications, whether mobile or web (Graham, 2011). They reward hackers by sending them gifts and certificates in addition to paying them. This culture of encouragement pushes the ethical hacker to perform further tests to secure the digital lives of billions of people.

Furthermore, these organizations publish hackers' names on a page called the "Hall of Fame." Each company has its own Hall of Fame, which celebrates and encourages cyber security enthusiasts around the globe and makes them valuable in the job market. The idea of rewarding hackers is known as Bounty Programs. Nowadays, many online platforms such as HackerOne provide the link between companies and white hats, simplifying the process of finding a company that rewards ethical hackers.

## The Rise of Cyber Warfare

After the wave of digitizing education, health, and even social interactions, wars have been digitized as well. Public opinion has begun to discuss a new concept: cyber warfare. Just as cyber security is a projection of physical security, cyber warfare is a projection, in a virtual environment, of the wars the world has always had. Countries have started to hire hackers for their cyber armies.

In the last two years, the media has reported on the hacking of the Pentagon's official website, the doctoring of the United States elections, and the attack against the Iranian nuclear centers (Holloway, 2015). At this point, we must distinguish between two types of attacks: organized and unorganized. The events mentioned above were organized attacks by the cyber armies of China, Russia, and the United States.

### China

China has hired between 50,000 and 100,000 citizens for its national cyber army. They have carried out several attacks against the United States, including the recent hacks of the Pentagon's and the White House's official websites. They also performed a "Denial of Service" attack that took the internet down for about twelve hours by targeting the most visited websites in the world, such as Facebook, Twitter, Google, GitHub, and Quora. It was one of the most horrific nightmares to befall the internet in the twenty-first century (Sher, 2016).

### Russia

Russia, on the other hand, has been building its own cyber army unofficially. Russian president Vladimir Putin claimed that patriotic hackers may have targeted the United States elections, but that they were not supported by the state. In an interview, he said: "Hackers are free people, just like artists who wake up in the morning in a good mood and start painting," according to The Atlantic.

If we dig into the deeper dimensions of the Russian president's speech, we can argue that, at the very least, Russia understands what cyber warfare is about. It recognizes that hackers have become an important variable in the equation of major powers. The Russian president publicly claimed that Russians may have doctored the United States elections, something that cannot be done with conventional weapons because it relied on social engineering to manipulate public opinion. Good evidence of this is what WikiLeaks published about Hillary Clinton a few days before the elections (Sher, 2016).

### The United States

In addition to China and Russia, the United States is another country that has started building its own cyber army. U.S. official Thomas Bossert said at a conference in Tel Aviv last month that the U.S. needs to switch from missiles to malware, in collaboration with Israel, by exchanging experience in this domain (Sher, 2016). Israel is an advanced country in cyber security. It created an operating system called Kali Linux for penetration testing, one of the most widely used operating systems in cyber security around the world. Israel has also built several cyber security laboratories, including the security lab of the famous company Intel in Haifa.
