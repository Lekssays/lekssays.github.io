I just finished reading a book called "A Hebrew Female in My Heart – في قلبي انثى عبرية" that amazed me. Unfortunately, there is no English version of this book; I would love to read it in English someday. My rating is 5/5. It was written by Dr. Khaoula Hamdi, a Tunisian author and a professor of information technology, a detail that surprised me when I first read it. Across 388 pages, she tells the story of a Hebrew girl caught between Lebanon and Tunisia, between Judaism, Islam, and Christianity, between war and peace, and between love and religion.

> If you have not read the book and do not want to be spoiled, please stop here until you finish it.

## A Mixture of Concepts

This compelling mixture of concepts, combined with the fact that the book tells a real story, pushed me to finish it as quickly as I could. The author's way of describing the events made it all the more engaging to read.

## The Story

A Hebrew girl named Nada, born to a Muslim father and a Jewish mother, initially lived in Tunisia and then moved to Lebanon after her parents divorced. Her mother married a Christian man who filled the role of the father in the lives of both Nada and her sister Dina.

One day she found two Muslim men at her door in a terrible state. Ahmed was injured, and his best friend Hassan was supporting him. Nada took care of Ahmed in the garage, even though it was forbidden because of the difference in their religions, and especially because of the war in southern Lebanon. They felt an unusual connection, yet with absolute respect for each other's religion in the context of men and women meeting at that time, a connection that would later build into a great story.

She faced many consecutive misfortunes:

- The death of Rima, her best friend, in the civil war in Qana, Lebanon. Rima had saved her life by teaching her Islam, alongside the lessons Ahmed gave to both of them. Nada was also wounded in that same war while searching for Rima.
- The disappearance of her lover Ahmed during one of the resistance's operations, a four-year absence in which Ahmed lost his memory. He lived in a village throughout that period, believing he was Christian.
- Being barred from living in her mother's home after she converted to Islam.
- The death of George, her spiritual father, and of her spiritual brother Michael, which took a heavy toll on her mental health.

This path changed her life, shaped by the books Ahmed gave her and by the behavior of Ahmed and Rima, both of which had a critical effect on her. In the end, she helped all of her family members and friends convert to Islam. She married Ahmed, and they still live in peace.

I have left out some events and some other people who played a great role in this story. I strongly encourage you to read it, because it pushed me to see things from different angles.

## What I Learned

I learned from this book that a religion should not be judged by the behavior of the people who believe in it. The concept of religion itself does not cause wars; in fact, political and economic agendas are the main cause of hate.

I learned that the media is a "make-up" tool that can make one religion appear more trustworthy than another. This, however, is based on personal opinions or on the agendas those media agencies serve, which makes the media a dangerous tool that shapes public opinion.

I concluded that religion should be taken as a well-formed set of noble ideas that cannot be interpreted through science, because science is not absolute while religion at its core can be seen as absolute. Comparing religion with science is therefore not a good path to follow. Religions can be compared with one another based on the concepts they promote, without being influenced by a third party that can manipulate the genuine opinion of any human being.

I noticed that people of different religions can live in peace if each one respects the other's ideas. This story illustrates an image that the media does not promote. It taught me how to value friendship and how to handle conflict between close friends. It also taught me how to forgive people and to take into account their particular circumstances at a given moment.
