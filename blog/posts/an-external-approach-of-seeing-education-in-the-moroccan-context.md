## Why I Care About Teaching

Since childhood, I have loved explaining concepts to my friends. I have loved writing on the board with my classmates seated behind me, and I never hesitate when someone asks for help understanding a topic. I also love reading what does not appear in the textbooks.

After years of learning the basics of computer science, I enriched this experience by volunteering to teach web development at a summer camp. It was one of the best experiences I have ever had, because I helped high school students build their own websites. It was unique: it taught me how to work with students, how to carry myself in a classroom, and how to explain a difficult concept in a fun and accessible way.

To understand the profession more deeply, I interviewed an associate professor at Al Akhawayn University in Ifrane. He was happy that a student wanted to ask about his job, because it gave him a chance to talk about the problems he faces every day. He asked me not to include his name in the report. I was glad to meet him, because I learned a great deal from a different mindset. The interview was as follows.

## The Interview

**Ahmed Lekssays:** *What happens during a typical day?*

**Professor:** I have a few hours of teaching, office hours, and independent research. Sometimes I have meetings with the administration about supervision, curriculum, and students. In addition, I have to work with students on internships, capstones, and exit tests.

**Q:** *What are the most important personal satisfactions and dissatisfactions connected with your occupation? What part of this job do you personally find most satisfying? Most challenging?*

**A:** Explaining a topic well and seeing that students truly understand it is my greatest satisfaction. On the other hand, it is a real dissatisfaction when a student does not grasp the point behind a topic. The most challenging part of my mission is explaining a topic that requires prerequisites and background information, because I have to cover both the prerequisites and the actual course material in a short period of time.

**Q:** *What kinds of problems do you deal with?*

**A:** Some of the problems I face in teaching are political problems with the administration, a lack of student motivation, and issues related to the modern U.S. educational system. That system reduces meeting time with students, so professors cannot fully play their mentorship role and therefore cannot influence their students. I believe a professor's role goes beyond what is stated in the textbook. Professors should shape their students in other aspects of life, such as ethics.

**Q:** *What are the major qualifications for success in this occupation?*

**A:** Success is relative, so I will answer according to my own definition. For me, success is making a difference in students' lives. The three keys to success in this mission are sincerity, dedication, and patience.

**Q:** *What special advice do you have for a student seeking to qualify for this position?*

**A:** First, do not take it as a job or merely a position that guarantees an income. Second, take your studies seriously. Finally, keep learning outside the classroom and beyond what textbooks state.

**Q:** *Do you have any special words of warning or encouragement as a result of your experience?*

**A:** From my experience, you should always question your intention. Why am I doing this? Is it for money, fame, or status? This will help you find the right reason. If your motivation is money, fame, or status, look for another mission. Teaching is not for you.

**Q:** *What kinds of experience, paid or unpaid, would you encourage for anybody pursuing a career in this field?*

**A:** Tutoring would be a great experience, especially if it is unpaid. By explaining a concept to someone, you benefit as well. You should treat it as a duty to help those around you understand a concept you are familiar with.

**Q:** *How has your job affected your lifestyle?*

**A:** It has affected it in many ways, but the important thing is that you keep learning and are always surrounded by young people, which improves your energetic outlook.

**Q:** *How do you see the value of professors in Morocco?*

**A:** Moroccan society shows great respect for professors in general. However, some professors do not take their job seriously, and they undermine those who are serious about their mission.

**Q:** *How do you see the role of professors in the development of nations?*

**A:** The role of professors is fundamental to the development of nations. However, as I said before, a professor should treat teaching as a duty and have an impact on his or her environment. For example, "At Taalim Al-Atik" is a good example of having an impact on students through daily meetings. The students learn how to deal with different situations; for instance, they learn how to respond to the teacher when he or she is angry.

**Q:** *Is there anything else you would like to share?*

**A:** I would like to keep my name hidden. I am also very disappointed about the status of professors around the world. Some professors teach for money, fame, or status, and as a result the work atmosphere has become negative.

## What I Took Away

My interviewee inspired, motivated, and influenced me, because the interview contained exactly what I was looking for. I needed to question myself in order to choose the right path, and he sees teaching the way I do. It is not only a job; it is a duty and a mission that can positively change a community. The main points I took from the interview are hard work, patience, and dedication.

### Hard Work

I learned that a professor is a hard worker who goes beyond what is stated in the syllabus or course material. A professor should be able to think outside the box in order to explain a concept to students, a skill that can be improved by seeking out different resources and analyzing different teaching strategies. Continuous learning is therefore not only about the field of study. It goes further, because it involves understanding students' backgrounds in terms of psychology, culture, and their ability to grasp information. A professor should be able to explain difficult concepts in a simple way, because the manner of explanation reflects how well the professor understands the concept. In addition, a professor should be open-minded, because sometimes students know what the professor does not, so information should flow in both directions.

### Patience

Teaching is also about patience. The professor plays a fundamental role in the community and is a source of knowledge that can build a knowledgeable society. A professor should have an impact on students, who represent the next generation. However, this is hard under the American educational system, because there is not enough meeting time to make an impact. The professor should be a role model in different aspects of life, because he or she is seen as a leader whom the community respects and follows. So the fundamental key for a student who wants to pursue a path in teaching is patience. It is not a job that pays materially. It pays in personal satisfaction and in an impact that goes beyond personal goals. Therefore, it should be seen as a duty, not a source of money.

### Dedication

Dedication is another fundamental key to building a successful career as a professor. A professor should love what he or she is doing and should fulfill that role without expecting a reward from the community. The best way for a student to experience this job is tutoring, especially when it is unpaid. The student learns how to give without expecting a reward. This may go unseen because it is not material, but it enriches self-satisfaction, which is my ultimate goal. Success can be seen from different perspectives, but I define it as reaching self-satisfaction by sharing knowledge with humanity.

## Conclusion

To sum up, being a professor is not an easy mission, because it involves many aspects, such as personality, community, and one's personal definition of success. The main keys a student needs in order to become a successful professor who has an impact on the community are patience, dedication, and hard work. Sharing and building knowledge is a noble thing, because it helps humanity along the path of development. This mission can be experienced through unpaid tutoring, which is a minimal version of teaching.
