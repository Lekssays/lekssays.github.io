## Introduction

Advertising is about targeting the masses. However, it is hard to target adults, because they think critically about the products they want to buy, so they know whether they need a product or not. After several studies of children's behavior, companies concluded that children are the weakest part of the community. As a result, companies today are targeting children. You can probably guess why.

According to the documentary, children are a major force because they push their parents to buy whatever seems attractive to them, even when they do not need it. They spend over $40 billion of their own money, and they push their parents to spend another $700 billion annually. The documentary compared this budget to the combined economies of the 115 poorest countries. In short, children are a powerful demographic for marketers.

In this paper, we will discuss the targeting of children by advertisers, the effect of advertising on children, and the objectification of men and women in the media, especially in TV shows and advertisements.

## Targeting Children Is Unethical

First, the documentary considers targeting children to be unethical for several reasons.

Psychologically speaking, children are easily influenced because they do not think critically about a product or an advertisement. They simply consume what they receive from the media, and they form relationships between themselves and their environment. For instance, if children see that their friends have a product they do not own, they will ask their parents to buy it, because they are influenced so easily.

The practice is also unethical because companies treat children as a vulnerable target. They care only about money, so they treat children as objects rather than as human beings.

Moreover, children follow celebrities, which means they are influenced by their actions. Children see celebrities as role models, so they always try to behave like them. Companies exploit this in several ways:

- They use celebrities to integrate children into a new culture that aims to increase corporate profits.
- They link celebrities to their products in order to build a strong connection between children and those products. This connection pushes parents to buy the products because their children's heroes use them.
- They play on emotions, because parents always try their best to make their children happy.

We can clearly see that companies do not consider the ethical dimension of this process.

## Gender Discrimination

The second main issue is gender discrimination between boys and girls in order to treat them as targets. Entertainment companies such as Disney try to build a culture that emphasizes the difference between girls and boys; they want boys' culture to be separated from girls' culture.

This new, industrialized culture aims to link boys with power. Boys' cartoons and programs are all about violence and power. On the other hand, companies want to link girls with concern for their bodies, especially makeup. In those programs, they transmit the image that a girl's ultimate goal is to look cute and attractive. At the same time, they try to convey that a successful boy is one who attracts many girls.

In this way, entertainment programs create gender discrimination, which is against both the laws and the ethics of media, because they use children's innocence for their own profit.

## Conclusion

To sum up, companies seek to increase their profit regardless of whether the means are ethical. They target a large and vulnerable population: children. Advertisements affect children's psychology because children lack the critical thinking skills needed to analyze what they watch in advertisements and TV shows. In addition, companies create a gender-discrimination culture by separating boys' culture from girls' culture in order to create two different markets and increase their profit. These actions raise urgent questions about the application of ethics in advertisements, especially those that involve children.
