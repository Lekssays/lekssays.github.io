By now, I am sure you have received plenty of emails about the new European Union regulation called GDPR, the General Data Protection Regulation. It is now recognized as law across the EU under 2016/679. The European Union is a leader in protecting the privacy and data of its citizens, and it pushes big companies to comply with this regulation. What makes GDPR especially interesting is that the penalties can reach up to **4%** of a company's total worldwide annual turnover.

## Why GDPR Was Needed

Technology has transformed our lives in ways nobody could have imagined, so a review of the rules was overdue.

## What Changed

In 2016, the EU adopted the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), one of its greatest achievements in recent years. It replaces the 1995 Data Protection Directive, which was adopted when the internet was still in its infancy.

## Compliance Timeline

Member States were given two years to ensure that GDPR was fully implementable in their countries by May 25th, 2018.

## Quick Facts

- The company **must be able to demonstrate that the user has consented** to the processing of his or her personal data.
- You have **the right to withdraw your consent** at any time.
- Before giving consent, you **must be informed** of it.
- Withdrawing consent **must be as easy as giving it**.
- The processing of personal data revealing **racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, or trade union membership, as well as the processing of genetic data, biometric data for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural person, data concerning health, or data concerning a natural person's sex life or sexual orientation**, is prohibited.
- The company must take appropriate measures to provide any information and communication relating to processing in a **concise**, **transparent**, **intelligible**, and **easily accessible** form, using **clear and plain language**.
- Right to Erasure, or the Right to be Forgotten: the user has the right to obtain from the company **the erasure of personal data concerning him or her without undue delay**, and the company has the obligation to erase that personal data without undue delay.
