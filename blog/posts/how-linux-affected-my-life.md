## More Than an Operating System

I have been using Linux-based operating systems since 2012, and over those years I built a strong relationship with my favorite one: Ubuntu. When I thought deeply about it back then, I realized that it is not just an operating system. GNU/Linux is a philosophy in computer science. It is an independent way of thinking.

This brings us to open source projects. Such projects show that great things are made by many people who love what they are doing. They are built on the contributions of millions of developers around the globe, people who differ in many aspects of life. They have different religions, different cultures, and different languages. Yet code, and the love of making people's lives better, unites them.

## How It Shaped My Thinking

Linux affected my way of thinking and my behavior toward both myself and my environment. It taught me that life is not all about money. Life is about helping other people, even if they do not know who you are or what you are doing.

Along the way, we gain good friends who share our interests, or who simply respect us because we are doing something they find valuable for themselves or for the community as a whole. Some people do not understand or value what I am doing, and they decide to leave because they think I have a boring, nerdy life. That is their problem, because I believe I am doing the right thing for my own satisfaction. Others encourage me every day and teach me every day, in every aspect of life, and they accept me as I am. To those people, I will prove that I am worthy of their confidence.

## How It Shaped My Decisions

Linux affected my decisions. When I want to make a decision, I think about it carefully, because Linux taught me that there is no going back to the previous state. When you type `sudo rm -rf /opt/folder`, there is no way (short of a forensics headache, of course) to return to the normal state. It taught me to be firm and precise in my decisions.

Linux also taught me to assume my responsibilities and to act professionally. Typing `sudo` at the beginning of any command reflects this. The system has two distinct states: the normal state and the superuser state. From this, I learned to separate professional life from personal life. The professional side needs careful management and strong, deliberate behavior. The personal side, on the other hand, does not decide on the most important matters, so it can be handled without extra stress or overthinking.

## How It Shaped My Emotions

Linux affected my emotions. I started to think in a binary way. I do not take or reconsider a decision twice. When I decide on something, I accept responsibility for the consequences it might have. In life, then, you either get 0 or 1; I do not believe in 0.5. Adopting this approach has caused repeated failures on my social and emotional side. Even so, it remains clear in my mind, because there is no middle state between love and hate.

## A Philosophy, Not Just Software

GNU/Linux is not just an operating system. It represents the way of thinking of millions of noble souls who made humanity great by connecting individuals and providing a wonderful platform for new technologies to emerge.
